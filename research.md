# 407 - Wholesale & B2B Commerce

**Date:** 2026-05-02

---

## 1. Overview

Wholesale and B2B commerce platforms enable manufacturers, distributors, and wholesalers to sell to business buyers through a digital channel that enforces the complexity of commercial relationships: negotiated price lists, customer-group-specific catalogues, order minimums and volume discounts, account hierarchies with buyer and approver roles, and payment terms such as net-30 or credit limits. Unlike direct-to-consumer ecommerce, B2B transactions typically involve repeat ordering of large quantities, procurement approval chains, and contractual pricing that differs by customer. The B2B ecommerce market continues to grow rapidly, with platforms at every price point from Shopify's newly extended B2B features through to purpose-built enterprise platforms.

---

## 2. Core Features

- **Customer-specific pricing:** Price lists tied to individual accounts or customer groups; contract pricing enforced across storefront, sales rep portal, and order management without manual spreadsheet management
- **Custom catalogues:** Restrict product visibility by customer group, geography, or channel; present different assortments to wholesale accounts versus retail buyers
- **Order minimums and quantity rules:** Enforce minimum order values, minimum line quantities, and case-pack multiples at checkout
- **Account and approval management:** Company profiles with org hierarchies; multiple buyer logins per account; configurable approval workflows for purchase orders above a threshold
- **Quick reorder and saved carts:** Buyers assemble, save, and resubmit standing orders; reduces friction for regular replenishment purchases
- **Trade credit and payment terms:** Net-30/60/90, credit limits, purchase order as payment method, and vaulted credit cards for approved accounts
- **Sales rep tools:** Representatives access customer accounts, place orders on behalf of buyers, and view account history

---

## 3. Market Landscape

- **Shopify B2B** — in 2026 Shopify extended native B2B features (company profiles, custom catalogs, volume discounts, payment terms) to merchants below the Plus tier, significantly democratising access
- **BigCommerce B2B Edition** — mid-market platform; strong support for customer groups, price lists, and B2B-specific checkout flows
- **OroCommerce** — open-source-based, purpose-built B2B platform; deep support for complex account hierarchies and procurement workflows; higher TCO but strong flexibility
- **Adobe Commerce (Magento)** — enterprise-grade; handles the most complex B2B scenarios but requires significant implementation investment
- **RepSpark** — wholesale ecommerce specialist for fashion and consumer goods brands; line sheet ordering and buyer portal
- **WizCommerce** — wholesale management software focused on mid-market distributors

---

## 4. Key Differentiators

- **Pricing enforcement:** The core differentiator is whether negotiated pricing is reliably enforced across every channel — web, mobile, EDI, and sales rep orders — without manual overrides
- **Procurement integration:** Enterprise buyers expect their purchase orders to originate from their own procurement systems (SAP Ariba, Coupa); EDI and punchout catalogue support is essential for large accounts
- **Self-service vs. assisted buying:** The mix of buyer self-service and sales rep assisted ordering varies by industry; platforms must support both modes with consistent data
- **Headless and API-first architecture:** B2B buyers increasingly access commerce through ERP integrations and mobile apps rather than web storefronts; API-first platforms support these custom touchpoints
- **Total cost of ownership:** Mid-market B2B platforms carry 3-year TCOs ranging from $100K to over $1M depending on customisation requirements; TCO modelling is essential during platform selection
